Advancing a Global Framework for Pandemic Risk Assessment

A review of 11 pandemic risk and preparedness tools, based on 59 documents and 27 expert consultations. The report sets out 12 design principles for a next-generation assessment.

02

Eleven tools in four families

The tools include WHO accountability instruments and Moody’s catastrophe models. Each covers part of the risk. Select a tool for details.

03

Four gaps in existing tools

Preparedness indices are descriptive and pathogen tools are narrow. Few models produce decision-ready outputs.

01

Static variables

Tools rely on fixed inputs and update irregularly, which hurts timeliness and prediction.

02

No subnational data

National averages hide local differences in risk and capacity.

03

Little political context

Trust, polarization and misinformation affect outbreaks and compliance. Tools barely include them.

04

Weak economic links

Tools rarely link risk to economic and fiscal impact, which finance ministries and funders use to decide.

04

Twelve design principles for a new tool

The principles come from the gaps in existing frameworks and from expert views on how the tools are used. They are grouped in three tiers.

05

What the report recommends

The report proposes a modular assessment ecosystem based in an independent institution. It would express risk in money and link it to actions agreed in advance.

Foundational

Governance and mandate

Share ownership across global, regional and national levels for legitimacy and buy-in. State the purpose publicly, publish the methods and show uncertainty as ranges.

Architecture

Subnational and sociopolitical data

Use local data even when incomplete. Treat trust and polarization as core determinants.

Architecture

Ecological drivers and live data

Put climate, land use and antimicrobial resistance at the center. Use a modular design with automated data feeds and versioned updates.

Outputs

Outputs for decision-makers

Frame outputs for each user and express risk as cost of inaction and ROI. Link risk tiers to predefined responses.

Tier 1Escalate surveillance
Tier 2Targeted investments
Tier 3Surge financing
Tier 4Activate playbooks

Each tier triggers a predefined action. The four actions are examples from the report.
